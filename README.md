# PHYS 321: Modern Physics

Course notes, worked examples, visualizations, and computational exercises for **PHYS 321: Modern Physics** at [East Texas A&M University](https://www.etamu.edu/), developed by **Billy Quarles**.

**[Read the online course notes](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/home.html)**

## Purpose

This Jupyter Book supports the transition from introductory physics to advanced study of relativity, quantum mechanics, and their applications. It emphasizes the relationship between experimental evidence and physical models, the development of symbolic solutions, and the interpretation of results.

Students develop their Python skills alongside the course material. Jupyter notebooks combine written reasoning, mathematical notation, calculations, and figures so that a solution can be explained, checked, and extended. Computational activities encourage students to predict behavior, vary parameters, and evaluate whether the results agree with the underlying physics.

## Course organization

- **Introduction:** The origins of modern physics, special relativity, early quantum experiments, and atomic structure.
- **Quantum Mechanics:** Wave functions, quantum models, the hydrogen atom, atomic physics, and statistical physics.
- **Applications of Modern Physics:** Molecules and lasers, semiconductors, nuclei, and elementary particles.

The course schedule determines the assigned sections and pacing.

## Using the material

The [course home page](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/home.html) explains learning goals, study habits, and homework expectations. Analytical and computational solutions follow the reasoning of scientific writing: **Introduction, Method, Results and Discussion, and Conclusion**, with explicit headings used when helpful. Short-answer questions require one coherent paragraph of approximately five complete sentences. See the [homework template](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/Preamble/HW_template.html) for examples, sentence minimums, formatting, and submission requirements.

The preamble provides references that students can revisit as their skills develop:

- [Who are these notes for?](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/Preamble/who-for.html)
- [Introduction to Python](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/Preamble/Python-basics.html)
- [Introduction to Markdown](https://saturnaxis.github.io/ModernPhysics/Preamble/Markdown-basics.html)

Read the rendered book online or open notebook chapters in Google Colab through the launch menu where available. Save a personal copy to edit and run the examples. The syllabus and individual assignments provide course policies, deadlines, and submission requirements.
